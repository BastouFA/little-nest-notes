The minimalist rules

  1. Register for the first 3 months, not the first 3 years. High chairs, toddler gear and big toys can wait. You will know much more by then.
  2. Pick one of each, then wait. Bottles, pacifiers and swaddles: babies have opinions. Register for one or two kinds, not a full set.
  3. Measure first. Stroller fold size, car trunk, doorway width, counter space. In an apartment, footprint matters more than features.
  4. Use a cash or group-gift fund for big items like a stroller or car seat, so guests can chip in instead of buying another blanket.
  5. Consumables are the best registry items. Diapers in a few sizes, wipes, and a diaper fund never go to waste.

Sleep

  • A safe sleep space (bassinet, play yard or crib) that meets current federal safety standards, with a firm, flat mattress made for it and fitted sheets only. In small spaces, a bassinet or compact play yard for the first months is enough.

What parents regret registering for

From Babylist's 2026 survey of parents, these come up again and again as unused or unnecessary:

  • Bottle warmers and wipe warmers. Warm water or room-temperature bottles usually work fine.
  • Complete bottle sets bought before baby tried them.
  • Sterilizers that don't wash. If you want an appliance, a washer-dryer does more.
  • Swings and bulky "container" gear, especially in small homes. Some babies love a swing; many never do. Borrow first.
  • Too many newborn clothes. Babies outgrow newborn size fast, and some skip it entirely.

Safety: what never goes on the list

Following American Academy of Pediatrics safe-sleep guidance and US CPSC rules: no crib bumpers, no inclined sleepers (anything inclined more than 10 degrees for sleep), no weighted sleep sacks, swaddles or blankets, no infant loungers or positioners for sleep, and no pillows or loose blankets in the crib. Loungers were among the most-recalled infant products of 2026. Before you register for anything, look it up at cpsc.gov/Recalls, and check car seats at nhtsa.gov/recalls.

FAQ

Which baby registry has the best completion discount?

As of November 2026, Babylist, Amazon and Target all offer 15%. Babylist's covers up to $4,000 of Babylist-sold items (up to $600 saved) and can be used across many orders. Amazon's covers up to $2,000 (up to $300). Target gives a 15% coupon you can use twice within six months. Terms change, so check each store's help page before you register.

Can I have more than one baby registry?

Yes. Many parents use Babylist as a universal registry and add items from Amazon, Target or any other store, so guests only need one link. You can also link an existing Amazon or Target registry to Babylist.

What baby items are a waste of money?

In Babylist's 2026 parent survey, the items most often called never used or regretted included bottle and wipe warmers, big sets of one bottle type bought before baby tried it, sterilizers that don't wash, and swings. Babies are all different, so register for a few basics and buy more once you know what works.

How many items should be on a baby registry?

There is no right number, but a minimalist registry is usually 30 to 60 items including consumables like diapers and wipes. Add a range of prices so every guest can find something, and use a cash or group-gift fund for big-ticket items.

When should I start a baby registry?

Most people start in the second trimester, around 16 to 20 weeks, so it is ready before shower invitations go out. Registries need to be active for 14 to 30 days before completion discounts apply.
