Do you even need a monitor?

The AAP recommends that babies sleep in their parents' room, on their own flat, firm sleep surface, for at least the first 6 months. If your baby sleeps a few feet from you at night, the monitor is mainly for naps and for the evening hours after baby goes down before you do.

That is why the cheap, simple monitors sell so well. In a one- or two-bedroom apartment you do not need 1,000 feet of range, sleep analytics or a $10 monthly plan. You need a clear picture, decent night vision, a parent unit that lasts through the evening, and sound alerts.

A smart monitor makes more sense when both parents want to check in from work, when a grandparent or sitter is at home with the baby, or when you genuinely enjoy sleep data.

WiFi vs non-WiFi: the real trade-off

Non-WiFi ("closed-loop") monitors pair a camera directly with its own handheld screen over a dedicated radio link. There is no app, no account, no cloud and nothing to log into, so there is nothing for a stranger on the internet to break into. The downside: you can only watch within range of your home, and the parent unit is one more thing to charge.

WiFi monitors stream to your phone from anywhere and usually add features like sleep tracking. They depend on your home WiFi, they need an account, and they carry the same security risks as any internet camera. In 2026 alone, researchers reported serious flaws in several cheap WiFi cameras sold for nurseries. The big brands have better security records, but if you go WiFi, set a strong unique password, turn on two-factor login and keep the firmware updated.

One more lesson from the market: "no subscription" can change. When the smart-monitor company Miku went bankrupt in 2023, the new owner moved features that used to be free behind a monthly fee. A closed-loop monitor cannot do that to you.

Safety: cords, placement and breathing trackers

  • Keep every cord at least 3 feet from the crib. Monitor cords are a strangulation hazard. Mount the camera on a wall or shelf, never on or over the crib rail where a baby could reach it.
  • Never use a monitor in place of safe sleep. Baby on their back, on a firm, flat surface, with nothing else in the crib.
  • Check recalls before you buy. In February 2026 about 81,800 Babysense Max View parent units were recalled because the display could overheat while charging. Look up any model at cpsc.gov/Recalls.
  • Secure WiFi cameras. Unique password, two-factor login, automatic firmware updates.

FAQ

Can a baby monitor without WiFi be hacked?

A closed-loop monitor sends video straight from the camera to its own parent screen over a dedicated radio link, with no internet account or cloud server. That removes the remote-hacking risk that WiFi cameras carry. The trade-off is that you cannot watch from your phone when you are away from home.

What is the range of a non-WiFi baby monitor?

Makers usually quote several hundred to about 1,000 feet in open air. Walls, floors and appliances cut that a lot, so expect less indoors. In an apartment or a normal house, range is rarely a problem.

Do breathing monitors like Owlet or Nanit prevent SIDS?

No. The American Academy of Pediatrics says not to use home breathing or oxygen monitors as a way to reduce SIDS risk. The Owlet Dream Sock is FDA-cleared to display pulse rate and oxygen readings for healthy babies aged 1 to 18 months, which is not the same thing as preventing SIDS. Safe sleep practices are what lower the risk.

Do I need a baby monitor if we room-share?

Not always. The AAP recommends room-sharing (without bed-sharing) for at least the first 6 months. Many parents in small apartments find a monitor only matters for naps and evenings after baby goes down first. A cheap audio or basic video monitor covers that.

Do I need a subscription for Nanit or Owlet?

Not for the basics. Nanit's live video and alerts work without a plan, and Owlet's live readings and notifications stay free in its app. The paid plans add history, trends and extra analysis. Check current plan details before buying, because they change.
