What the hospital usually gives you (so you don't buy it twice)

Many US hospitals send you home with mesh underwear, large pads, a basic peri bottle, ice packs and sometimes witch-hazel pads or numbing spray. Policies vary, so ask on your hospital tour or at a prenatal visit: "What postpartum supplies do you provide, and can I take extras home?" Then buy only what fills the gaps.

The hospital bag checklist

Pack this around 36 weeks. If you have a scheduled C-section, pack a week earlier.

For you

  • High-waisted underwear (disposable or soft cotton) that clears the incision line
  • Loose nursing nightgown or a robe, plus high-waist joggers
  • Soft nursing or sleep bra
  • Slip-on shoes or slides (bending to tie laces is no fun)
  • A 10-foot phone charging cable (hospital outlets are never near the bed)
  • Lip balm, hair ties, toiletries, glasses if you wear contacts
  • Nipple balm and a few nursing pads if you plan to breastfeed
  • A small pillow to hold against your belly in the car and when you cough
  • Going-home outfit that doesn't press on your abdomen (think dress or high-waist leggings)
  • Snacks and a water bottle with a straw (easier when you can't sit up)

For baby

  • An installed, rear-facing infant car seat: the hospital won't let you leave without one. Have the install checked by a certified technician (find one at cert.safekids.org).
  • One or two going-home outfits in newborn and 0–3 month sizes
  • A swaddle or blanket for the car-seat ride (over the straps, never under them)

Paperwork

  • ID, insurance card, birth plan if you have one, pediatrician's name and number

C-section recovery essentials, explained

High-waist postpartum underwear

After a C-section, regular underwear tends to sit right on the incision. High-waist disposable or soft stretch underwear sits above it and holds a pad in place. Frida Mom makes a C-section-specific high-waist version; plenty of parents also just size up in soft high-rise cotton briefs. Buy one pack of disposables for the first week, then switch to washable.

Upside-down peri bottle

You will bleed after a C-section too, and many people use a peri bottle to rinse after using the toilet. The angled spout on Frida Mom's version is the reason it's popular: it works while you're sitting, without twisting. It costs around $10–14. If your hospital's free bottle works for you, you can skip it.

Postpartum belly binder (optional, ask first)

Some people say a soft compression binder makes getting up, coughing and walking feel more manageable in the first weeks, and some hospitals offer one after surgery. It isn't required and it doesn't "flatten" your stomach. Ask your OB or surgeon whether it suits you and when to start. Stop if it hurts or irritates the incision.

Small things that make a big difference

  • A pillow to brace your belly when you cough, laugh or ride in the car. Any small firm pillow works.
  • A grabber or a basket by the bed so you aren't reaching or bending for water, snacks, diapers and your phone.
  • Stool softener: ask your doctor. Many hospitals give one after surgery.
  • Loose, high-waist clothing for at least the first few weeks.

If you have a vaginal birth

Most of the list stays the same. Swap the high-waist underwear for any comfortable postpartum briefs, and add perineal cooling: pad liners with witch hazel, ice packs (often free at the hospital) and a peri bottle. A Frida Mom kit covers most of this in one box.

What to skip (or at least wait on)

  • A full postpartum "haul" before birth. Buy the basics; get the rest delivered in the first week once you know what you need.
  • Waist trainers or "snap-back" shapewear. A medical binder, if your doctor suggests one, is different from a cosmetic waist trainer.
  • Lots of nursing bras in one size. Your size usually changes in the first weeks. Buy one or two, then more later.
  • Infant loungers and positioners. They are not safe for sleep, and loungers were among the most-recalled infant products of 2026.

Warning signs: when to call

Call your OB or midwife, or get urgent care, for: bleeding that soaks a pad in an hour or large clots; a fever; an incision that is red, hot, swelling, opening or leaking; severe headache or vision changes; chest pain or trouble breathing; one leg swollen, red or painful; or thoughts of harming yourself or your baby. In the US, the National Maternal Mental Health Hotline is free and confidential, 24/7: call or text 1-833-TLC-MAMA (1-833-852-6262). In a crisis, call or text 988.

FAQ

What should I pack in my hospital bag for a C-section?

High-waisted underwear that sits above the incision, a loose nursing nightgown or high-waist joggers, a long phone charger, lip balm, slip-on shoes, a supportive nursing or sleep bra, toiletries and a going-home outfit that doesn't press on your belly. Many hospitals provide pads, mesh underwear and a basic peri bottle, so ask what yours supplies.

Do I need a belly binder after a C-section?

Some people find a postpartum binder makes moving, coughing and getting out of bed feel more comfortable, and some hospitals offer one. It is not required. Ask your OB or surgeon whether and when to use one, and stop if it causes pain or irritation.

Is the Frida Mom recovery kit worth it?

It is convenient, especially as a gift, but it overlaps with what many hospitals hand out. If you are on a budget, buy the upside-down peri bottle and a pack of high-waist disposable underwear on their own, and take home the hospital's supplies.

When should I call my doctor after birth?

Call your OB or midwife, or get urgent care, for heavy bleeding (soaking a pad an hour), fever, a red, hot or leaking incision, severe headache, chest pain, trouble breathing, leg swelling or pain, or thoughts of harming yourself or your baby. In the US you can call or text the National Maternal Mental Health Hotline at 1-833-TLC-MAMA (1-833-852-6262) any time, or call 988 in a crisis.
